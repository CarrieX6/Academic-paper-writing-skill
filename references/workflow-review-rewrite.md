# Review and Rewrite Workflow

## Review at three levels

### Macro review

Check whether the paper's research question, gap, target/estimand, method, experiment, and conclusion refer to the same object. Identify claims unsupported by evidence or contradicted by completed work.

### Meso review

Reverse-outline sections and paragraphs. Check unique section jobs, paragraph necessity, concept order, transitions, and cross-section duplication.

### Micro review

Only after the first two levels, check topic/control sentences, syntax, terminology, notation, citation placement, English variety, and concision.

## Produce actionable review output

Lead with an overall verdict, then report:

- **Critical:** invalidates the thesis, evidence, novelty, ethics, or venue compliance.
- **Major:** breaks an important inferential link, section function, comparison, or reader understanding.
- **Minor:** local clarity, style, notation, or formatting issue.

For each issue give:

`location | diagnosis | why it matters | evidence | concrete repair`

Do not equate length with a problem. Identify the duplicated or missing function.

## Rewrite structurally

Before editing, state internally what must remain unchanged:

- verified facts and results;
- formulas, symbols, and formal assumptions;
- citation meaning and provenance;
- contribution boundaries and limitations;
- target audience and venue constraints.

Then:

1. repair the argument map;
2. reorder, split, merge, or delete paragraphs;
3. rewrite controlling sentences and evidence flow;
4. line-edit for precision and language consistency;
5. re-run claim-evidence and citation checks;
6. list unresolved issues rather than hiding them.

Prefer full-paragraph reconstruction over sentence-by-sentence synonym replacement when the paragraph has multiple jobs.

## Convert between conference and journal forms

Do not treat conversion as expansion or compression alone.

For conference to journal, determine what new scientific value and evidence justify the extension: broader validation, deeper analysis, stronger theory, external validity, or a substantially expanded question. Reorganize Results and Discussion as the journal expects and disclose prior versions according to policy.

For journal to conference, narrow the thesis and retain the minimum complete evidence chain. Move only genuinely secondary detail to an allowed supplement; do not hide central limitations or controls.

In either direction, re-check current policies on overlap, prior publication, anonymization, supplementary material, and citation of earlier versions.

## Perform adversarial deletion tests

- If deleting a paragraph loses nothing, remove or merge it.
- If deleting a citation changes no support, ask whether it is decorative.
- If deleting a method component leaves the claimed mechanism unchanged, reassess whether the component is core.
- If deleting an experiment leaves a contribution unsupported, the experiment is central and belongs in the main evidence chain.
- If deleting a limitation makes the headline appear stronger, narrow the headline rather than burying the limitation.

## Preserve uncertainty during rewriting

Never strengthen:

- association into causation;
- predictive improvement into clinical utility;
- one setting into generality;
- a non-significant result into equivalence;
- a preliminary result into a completed contribution;
- a citation's abstract-level suggestion into a verified detailed finding.

When evidence is incomplete, improve the structure around the gap and label it; do not improve the apparent certainty.
