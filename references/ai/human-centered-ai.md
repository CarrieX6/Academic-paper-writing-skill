# Human-Centered AI and HCI

Begin by naming the epistemic aim: estimating an effect, describing behavior, understanding experience or practice, developing theory, evaluating a design, or integrating several aims. Do not force all HCI work into a predictive-accuracy or hypothesis-testing template.

## Shared study checks

- Define the participant population, recruitment channel, inclusion/exclusion, setting, task, AI system/version, interaction protocol, incentives, and unit of analysis.
- Separate statements about sampled participants and tasks from claims about users, organizations, or long-term deployment. Convenience samples and brief laboratory exposure require bounded conclusions.
- Report ethics review or exemption, consent, privacy, sensitive-data handling, deception/debriefing, accessibility, and researcher or participant risk as applicable.
- Account for learning, novelty, fatigue, ordering, prior AI familiarity, noncompliance, attrition, and repeated observations when these can affect the conclusion.
- Distinguish perceived usefulness, trust, preference, reliance, behavior, performance, and welfare. Self-report on one construct does not establish another.

## Quantitative studies

- Define outcomes, comparisons, assignment, exclusions, stopping, and confirmatory versus exploratory analyses before interpreting significance.
- Match the model and uncertainty to clustering, repeated measures, missingness, ordinal scales, multiple outcomes, and the actual randomization unit.
- Report effect sizes and uncertainty, not only thresholded significance. Justify sample size or precision relative to the intended inference.
- Establish that scales and behavioral measures validly operationalize the construct in the studied population and context; internal consistency alone is insufficient.
- Avoid causal language for observational or self-selected exposure without a credible identification strategy.

## Qualitative studies

- State the methodological orientation and explain how sampling, data generation, analysis, and claims follow from it. Do not impose saturation, inter-rater agreement, or member checking as universal requirements across incompatible traditions.
- Describe who collected and analyzed the material, researcher positionality or reflexivity when relevant, coding or interpretive process, use of software, and how disagreements or alternative readings were handled.
- Provide an auditable link from data to themes or interpretations while protecting participants. Quotations illustrate analytic claims; they do not turn frequency into prevalence.
- Discuss negative cases, ambiguity, and the limits of transferability rather than presenting themes as population estimates.

## Mixed-methods studies

- State why integration is necessary, which component has priority, and whether the sequence is convergent, explanatory, exploratory, or another justified design.
- Connect sampling, timing, constructs, and units across components. Do not present two parallel studies as “mixed methods” without integration.
- Show where qualitative and quantitative evidence converges, complements, or conflicts, and explain how that affects the final inference.
- Preserve the validity standards of each component; one method does not automatically repair weaknesses in the other.
