# Chinese Doctoral Dissertation Mode

Use this mode for a doctoral dissertation written primarily in Chinese and governed by a Chinese degree-granting institution. It extends the common claim–evidence and citation-integrity rules; it does not replace institutional requirements.

## First principle: a dissertation is not a long journal article

A doctoral dissertation must demonstrate a coherent body of research and make the candidate's original contribution auditable across the whole document. Its central chain is:

`overall research problem -> linked subquestions -> chapter-level contributions -> evidence -> cross-chapter synthesis -> bounded doctoral contribution`

Do not obtain length by repeating background, methods, or results. Do not concatenate papers and call the result a dissertation. Conversely, do not remove necessary methodological detail, cross-chapter synthesis, or contribution attribution merely to imitate a concise paper.

Treat these as independent variables:

- dissertation language and any required bilingual components;
- degree-granting institution, graduate school, school/department, discipline, and degree type;
- dissertation form: monograph, publication-based, or hybrid;
- stage: proposal, progress review, drafting, pre-examination, blind review, defense, post-defense revision, or archive deposit;
- confidentiality, intellectual-property, ethics, data, and publication constraints.

If any variable is unknown, record it as unresolved. Do not infer a rule from another university, an old thesis, or a journal template.

## Verify the permitted dissertation form

Use a dissertation form only when the current institutional and disciplinary rules permit it.

### Monograph form

A monograph develops one cumulative argument through purpose-built chapters. Individual research chapters may originate from papers, but the dissertation's terminology, methods, evidence, and discussion must be integrated at thesis level.

### Publication-based form

A publication-based dissertation may organize peer-reviewed or submitted works as research chapters only when the institution permits the form and the included works meet its status, authorship, contribution, and formatting rules. It still requires an original general introduction, explicit links among the works, a defensible account of the candidate's contribution, and an integrative synthesis. It is not a container of publisher PDFs.

### Hybrid form

A hybrid dissertation combines purpose-built chapters with adapted publication-derived chapters. Use it only when permitted, and label the provenance, candidate contribution, overlap, and adaptation status of each chapter consistently.

Do not assume that publication acceptance, first authorship, co-first authorship, or journal prestige automatically establishes a doctoral contribution. The dissertation must make the candidate's intellectual ownership and the cumulative advance explicit.

## Establish the authority hierarchy before formatting

For the applicable stage, collect and date the current authoritative sources. A useful default hierarchy is:

1. applicable law and binding national or regional degree requirements;
2. university or graduate-school degree regulations, dissertation specification, and official template;
3. school, department, discipline, or degree-committee requirements that validly specialize the university rules;
4. official blind-review, defense, degree-application, and library/archive instructions for the current cycle;
5. ethics approvals, data agreements, funder conditions, publisher licenses, and coauthor agreements governing the underlying work;
6. supervisor or laboratory preferences;
7. exemplary dissertations, which reveal conventions but do not create rules.

Do not hard-code the hierarchy when the institution states a different one. Resolve conflicts through the responsible institutional office rather than silently choosing the convenient rule. Record:

`requirement | authority/source | version or date | stage | dissertation location | status | unresolved action`

Verify rather than assume requirements for citation style, word or character limits, chapter order, publication prerequisites, similarity checks, signatures, file format, anonymity, fonts, margins, numbering, bilingual metadata, and archive deposit. A national or disciplinary standard applies only when the institution adopts or requires it.

Fixed declarations, authorization forms, and signature pages must use the institution's exact approved wording. Do not paraphrase them for style.

## Create a dissertation-level argument map

Before substantial drafting or restructuring, maintain a map with at least:

`overall question | scope/population | subquestion | chapter | chapter claim | candidate's original contribution | evidence | dependency | limitation | status`

The map must answer all of the following:

- What single research problem makes the chapters belong in one dissertation?
- Which subquestion does each research chapter answer?
- What new knowledge, method, resource, theory, or validated finding is attributable to the candidate?
- What evidence supports each claimed contribution, and where is it reported?
- Which chapters depend on shared data, methods, assumptions, or intermediate findings?
- Which conclusions are cumulative across chapters and which remain study-specific?
- What evidence would weaken or falsify the overall thesis?

Every substantial research chapter should have a necessary role. If deleting a chapter does not change the overall answer, contribution, boundary, or evidential chain, justify its inclusion or revise the architecture. A chapter can be valuable as foundation, validation, negative evidence, or boundary analysis; it need not report a separate positive result.

Keep a claim–evidence ledger at two resolutions:

- **dissertation level:** overall claims, cumulative contributions, and scope boundaries;
- **chapter level:** local claims, data, methods, analyses, uncertainty, and limitations.

Do not promote a chapter-level finding to a dissertation-wide conclusion without an explicit synthesis argument.

## Design chapter and cross-chapter architecture

Section names and order are institution-dependent. Preserve the intellectual jobs below even when the template merges or renames them.

### General introduction

The general introduction should:

- establish the broad research context for a reader outside the narrow subtopic;
- define the overall problem, scope, central concepts, and information setting;
- synthesize the cross-cutting research gap rather than copy a paper introduction;
- state the overall question and linked subquestions;
- explain why the chapters form a cumulative program;
- preview the dissertation-level contributions without overstating unfinished or chapter-local findings;
- give a chapter roadmap that explains logical function, not merely chapter titles.

Do not make the reader reconstruct the overarching thesis from several local introductions.

### Background or literature synthesis

Use a standalone background or literature chapter only when it provides common foundations or a thesis-level synthesis that individual chapters cannot efficiently supply. Distinguish:

- common concepts and field-wide gaps needed by multiple chapters;
- chapter-specific nearest work needed to position a local contribution;
- literature used later to interpret cumulative findings.

Avoid both extremes: repeating the same review in every chapter, or placing all related work in one remote chapter so that local novelty boundaries become unclear.

### Shared foundations or general methods

A shared data, theory, or methods chapter is useful when several studies rely on the same cohort, apparatus, notation, preprocessing, ethical framework, or analytic protocol. State what is common and what changes by study. Research chapters should cross-reference this material and restate only what is necessary for local interpretation and reproducibility.

### Research chapters

Each research chapter should make its local chain visible:

`connection to overall problem -> local question/gap -> approach -> evidence -> local answer -> limitation -> bridge to the next chapter`

For a chapter adapted from a publication, replace journal-dependent context with dissertation-level context, harmonize notation and terminology, expose omitted dependencies, and state provenance and candidate contribution as required. Do not preserve duplicated abstracts, publisher layouts, reference lists, or paper-specific claims merely because they appeared in print.

### Integrative discussion or synthesis

The integrative discussion is not a sequence of chapter summaries. It should:

- answer the overall question using evidence across chapters;
- explain convergence, disagreement, complementarity, and dependency among findings;
- distinguish replicated evidence from multiple analyses of the same underlying data;
- compare the cumulative contribution with the closest literature;
- identify alternative explanations and threats to validity;
- state generalizability and transfer boundaries;
- separate established implications from future hypotheses.

If findings conflict, preserve the conflict and analyze its cause; do not manufacture a single positive narrative.

### Overall conclusion

The conclusion should give a concise, evidence-bounded answer to the overall question, enumerate the candidate's defensible cumulative contributions, and state the resulting scope. Do not introduce new evidence, a new central theory, or unsupported social, clinical, or industrial impact.

## Control necessary repetition

Some repetition is functional in a dissertation, especially when chapters must remain locally readable or are adapted from papers. Permit repetition when it serves a different intellectual job, such as:

- introducing a concept broadly in the general introduction and operationalizing it locally;
- summarizing a shared method before stating chapter-specific deviations;
- reporting a local result in a research chapter and integrating it with other results later;
- resetting notation or cohort definitions needed to read a semi-standalone chapter.

For repeated material, apply four tests:

1. **Function:** does the later occurrence do a different job?
2. **Resolution:** is it shorter, more detailed, or more synthetic as appropriate?
3. **Navigation:** can a cross-reference replace part of it without harming comprehension?
4. **Consistency:** do definitions, sample counts, claims, and terminology agree across occurrences?

Delete repetition that only reproduces wording or length. Do not force zero repetition when that would make chapters unintelligible.

## Make originality and authorship auditable

Distinguish the originality of the dissertation from the originality of each publication and from first authorship. Maintain a chapter-level contribution record covering, where applicable:

`problem conception | study design | data/resource creation | methodology | software | analysis | validation | visualization | writing | supervision/project administration`

For every coauthored or collaborative chapter:

- identify the candidate's specific intellectual and practical contributions;
- identify material contributions by coauthors and collaborators;
- disclose shared, co-first, or corresponding authorship accurately;
- distinguish work completed before candidature, during candidature, and outside the doctoral project when rules require it;
- use the institution's required declaration, approval, or contribution-table format;
- obtain required coauthor or supervisor confirmations.

A standard contributor taxonomy may assist attribution, but it does not override the institution's definition of an original doctoral contribution. Do not claim ownership of shared data, code, ideas, or text beyond the candidate's documented role.

## Govern reuse of published and submitted material

Treat text reuse, scientific attribution, and copyright permission as separate checks. Translation between Chinese and English does not erase textual overlap, attribution duties, or copyright restrictions.

For every included or adapted work, record:

`chapter/material | source citation | publication status | candidate role | coauthor approval | version used | license/copyright holder | permission/exception | required notice | adaptation | repository/embargo constraint`

Check at least:

- whether the institution permits inclusion of published, accepted, submitted, preprint, or unpublished material;
- whether the author-accepted manuscript, version of record, figures, tables, questionnaires, or third-party assets may be deposited in a public thesis repository;
- whether publisher or rights-holder permission and a prescribed acknowledgement are required;
- whether coauthors must approve inclusion or adaptation;
- whether overlapping articles and chapters are cited and disclosed transparently;
- whether reuse of the same cohort, experiment, figure, or analysis creates duplicate-evidence or salami-publication concerns;
- whether confidentiality, patent, data-use, sponsor, or embargo terms restrict examination or public deposit.

Do not use a publisher PDF by default. Do not treat citation alone as copyright permission, or permission as sufficient scholarly attribution. When rights are unresolved, use original reformulation or a rights-cleared version only if scientifically accurate and institutionally permitted; otherwise mark the material blocked and seek authoritative guidance.

## Apply the Chinese-language layer

Read [chinese-academic-writing.md](chinese-academic-writing.md) for Chinese prose, terminology, typography, bilingual front matter, and reference handling. Dissertation-specific invariants are semantic alignment of Chinese and English titles, abstracts, and keywords; consistency of terms and symbols across chapters; and obedience to the institution's adopted standard rather than a presumed national or journal style.

## Verify front matter and back matter

Build the exact order and required wording from the current official specification. Possible components include, but are not guaranteed to include:

- covers and title pages;
- originality, intellectual-property, and repository-authorization declarations;
- Chinese and English abstracts and keywords;
- table of contents and lists of figures, tables, abbreviations, symbols, or terminology;
- ethics, funding, conflicts, data/code availability, or author-contribution statements;
- references and appendices;
- publication/research-output list and relation of outputs to dissertation chapters;
- acknowledgments and candidate biography;
- defense or degree-committee approval pages.

Do not add, remove, rename, sign, translate, or reorder official components by analogy with another institution. Check whether front/back matter counts toward word, page, anonymity, or similarity limits. Ensure automatically generated contents, cross-references, figure/table lists, numbering, and page numbering match the final document.

## Route by degree stage

Stage names and required procedures vary. Apply only the stages recognized by the institution.

### Proposal or candidacy stage

Separate planned contributions from completed evidence. Establish feasibility, ethics/data access, research questions, dependency risks, and criteria for revising the program. Do not write future work as achieved results.

### Progress review and drafting

Update the dissertation-level map when studies, publications, or conclusions change. Preserve negative or inconclusive results that alter the program. Audit terminology, data versions, cohort counts, methods, and claims across chapters continuously rather than only at the end.

### Pre-examination and blind review

Create an anonymized derivative while preserving a complete master. Follow the exact blind-review policy for author names, supervisor names, affiliations, acknowledgments, funding, self-citations, publication lists, file names, document properties, repository links, code, and identifiable ethics/project information. Do not over-anonymize scientific detail unless required. Render and inspect the submitted file rather than relying on source-text search alone.

### Defense preparation

Map likely questions to the overall thesis, strongest evidence, weakest assumptions, candidate contribution, and limitations. Keep a response matrix:

`comment/question | direct answer | evidence | affected claim | dissertation change | status`

Distinguish clarification from a new analysis or changed conclusion. Integrate substantive changes into the dissertation rather than leaving them only in slides or oral answers.

### Post-defense revision and archive deposit

Verify that required committee changes are complete and traceable. Recheck signed/approved forms, final metadata, author names, bilingual titles and abstracts, bookmarks, links, fonts, figures, accessibility, file format, file size, permissions, embargoes, supplementary assets, and repository visibility according to official rules. The defended version and archived version may differ only through authorized changes; preserve a version record.

## Run a two-layer final audit

Neither layer can substitute for the other.

### Layer 1: doctoral scholarly closure

Audit both dissertation and chapter resolution:

- one coherent overall problem and a defensible cumulative answer;
- necessary chapter roles and explicit cross-chapter dependencies;
- traceable candidate-original contributions;
- claims matched to completed evidence, uncertainty, and limitations;
- consistency of data, methods, definitions, terminology, figures, and conclusions;
- honest treatment of negative findings, conflicting evidence, and external-validity boundaries;
- integrative discussion that adds synthesis rather than summary;
- citation, provenance, contribution, and reuse integrity.

Classify material claims as `supported`, `partially supported`, `planned`, `exploratory`, `contradicted`, or `unsupported`. Narrow or remove claims that do not close.

### Layer 2: degree and deposit compliance

Audit the current stage against authoritative sources:

- permitted dissertation form and publication/author-contribution eligibility;
- official template, structure, length, language, and bibliography requirements;
- ethics, data, confidentiality, intellectual-property, license, and permission status;
- exact declarations, signatures, approvals, and required supporting materials;
- blind-review anonymity or final-version identity, as applicable;
- similarity-check, defense-revision, archive, embargo, and repository requirements;
- rendered-document integrity, including metadata and machine-generated lists.

Report the final state separately as:

`scholarly closure: pass / conditional / fail`

`institutional compliance: verified / partially verified / unresolved / fail`

Do not call a dissertation submission-ready unless scholarly closure passes and all applicable compliance requirements are verified. When an institutional rule cannot be confirmed, name the unresolved authority and action rather than filling the gap with a generic academic-writing convention.
