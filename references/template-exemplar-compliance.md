# Venue, Institution, Template, and Exemplar Compliance

## Treat the current governing instructions as the authority

When a target and stage are fixed, perform a current compliance check before final drafting or typesetting. Identify the authority type first.

For a conference or journal paper:

1. Locate the venue's current official author instructions, call for papers, submission checklist, and official template or submission portal guidance.
2. Record the venue, track or article type, submission stage, version/year, and access date.
3. Confirm page or word limits, reference treatment, anonymity, supplement policy, abstract limits, required sections/declarations, file formats, and policy constraints.
4. Obtain the template from the official venue, publisher, or linked repository—not an unrelated mirror.
5. Verify whether the repository already uses that template and whether it matches the current cycle and submission stage.
6. Compile or render the manuscript and inspect warnings, overflow, font embedding, figure legibility, bibliography, and anonymization when feasible.

For a Chinese doctoral dissertation:

1. Locate the current university and graduate-school degree regulations, the degree program or faculty supplements, the authorized dissertation template, examination rules, and library or repository deposit requirements.
2. Record the institution, degree type, faculty/program, dissertation form, stage, rule/template version, and access date.
3. Resolve the stated hierarchy among binding national or regional requirements, university, graduate school, faculty/department, library, and template instructions; do not confuse a generic convention with a binding rule.
4. Confirm required language, title and abstract forms, keywords, chapter/front/back matter, numbering, reference style, originality and authorization declarations, publication/contribution lists, anonymization, examination copies, file formats, metadata, and deposit requirements.
5. Verify whether included published material is permitted and what version, attribution, co-author statement, copyright permission, and modification constraints apply.
6. Compile or render the complete dissertation and inspect contents lists, cross-references, numbering, bibliography, fonts, figures, equations, PDF metadata, required declarations, and archival file requirements when feasible.

Record the governing authority and template status as `verified_current`, `unverified_current`, `provisional_previous_cycle`, `mismatched`, `free_format`, or `not_applicable`. If the next cycle's or current degree stage's official instructions are unavailable, an older template is provisional and must not be described as current-compliant.

If official sources cannot be reached, record `unverified_current`, preserve the exact URL and failed check, and continue only with content work that does not depend on the unresolved rule. Do not claim compliance, perform final layout optimization, or substitute memory, a mirror, or an accepted paper as authority. Re-run the official check before submission-ready handoff.

If the current document class or style belongs to another venue, institution, degree type, or stage, do not present the document as submission- or deposit-ready. Resolve or obtain author approval for migration before a formatting/finalization task; for a content-only review, continue only while explicitly flagging the mismatch.

Do not rely on memory for changing rules. Do not treat an old accepted paper as a substitute for current instructions.

## Check AI-assistance policy before drafting

Venue, publisher, institution, degree-program, funder, or collaborator policies may require disclosure, limit reference generation, or prohibit AI drafting for particular document types. Before substantive AI-assisted writing:

1. inspect the current governing policy for the document type and stage;
2. classify the requested action as permitted, permitted with disclosure, or prohibited;
3. record any required AI-use statement and submission-stage placement;
4. stop any prohibited drafting, rewriting, image, review, or reference operation rather than assuming disclosure cures it;
5. remind the authors that they remain responsible for accuracy, attribution, confidentiality, and policy compliance.

Do not upload confidential manuscripts, unpublished examination materials, patient information, or restricted research artifacts to an external service when venue, institutional, consent, or collaboration rules prohibit it.

If no venue or degree institution has been selected, a target-specific AI policy cannot yet be evaluated. Subject to known institutional, collaborator, confidentiality, and data-governance rules, venue-neutral analysis or drafting may proceed, but record the policy status as `not_yet_applicable` and require a fresh check before target-shaped drafting or submission. If a target is fixed but its policy cannot be verified, pause only the actions whose permissibility depends on that unresolved policy and continue clearly separable read-only or non-target-dependent work.

## Use LaTeX only when appropriate

- If the venue requires or supplies a LaTeX template and the manuscript is in LaTeX, use the official class/style and sample structure.
- If the journal allows free-format initial submission, do not force a publisher template merely for appearance; still meet content and file requirements.
- For a Chinese doctoral dissertation, use the institution's authorized Word/LaTeX template or submission system when one is required; do not substitute a visually similar community template without marking it unverified.
- If the venue requires Word, a web form, or another format, do not convert to LaTeX by default.
- For outline-only or argument-review tasks, template migration is usually unnecessary; identify future compliance work instead.
- When revising an existing official-template manuscript, preserve its class, style files, and semantic macros unless the user requests migration.

Never shrink margins, fonts, line spacing, caption size, bibliography spacing, or style definitions to evade limits. Do not edit official style files unless the governing venue or institution explicitly instructs authors to do so.

## Build a target-appropriate exemplar corpus

Official rules specify compliance; well-chosen accepted papers or institution-approved dissertations reveal rhetorical norms that rules do not encode. When substantial drafting, venue conversion, or dissertation integration is requested, inspect a small, purposeful corpus rather than copying a fixed number of documents.

Choose exemplars that are:

- officially accepted, publisher-hosted, or institutionally deposited;
- recent enough to reflect the current venue, while including a canonical older paper only when structurally relevant;
- the same article type or track;
- close in problem type, method/evidence balance, and intended audience;
- diverse enough to avoid mistaking one author's preference for venue convention.

For a dissertation corpus, prefer recent successfully deposited dissertations from the same institution, degree type, and broad research tradition. Treat them as examples of realized structure, not proof that every feature remains permitted or scientifically appropriate.

Extract high-level patterns:

- abstract moves and information density;
- where the gap, method, and main result appear;
- section order and approximate allocation of attention;
- integration or separation of Related Work, Results, and Discussion;
- theorem/algorithm presentation;
- figure, table, and caption conventions;
- placement of limitations, ethics, reproducibility, and supplement pointers.

Do not copy sentences, rhetorical flourishes, citation counts, or visual designs. Do not infer a formal rule from one paper. Official instructions override exemplars.

## Cite content, not imitation

Documents consulted only to understand venue or institutional style normally do **not** need to be cited. Cite an exemplar when its scientific idea, definition, method, data, result, or claim is substantively used or discussed. Make that citation support the nearby sentence; do not cite it merely to signal familiarity or imitate an approved dissertation.

## Maintain a compliance record

For a final or near-final paper or dissertation, preserve an internal record:

`authority | requirement | official source | checked date | document location | status | action`

At minimum audit:

- correct track/article type and template version;
- title/abstract/keyword constraints;
- page or word limits and reference counting;
- anonymity and hidden PDF/file metadata;
- author, affiliation, acknowledgment, funding, and conflict rules for the stage;
- ethics, consent, IRB, data/code, model, and generative-AI disclosures where applicable;
- permitted AI-assistance scope and required disclosure;
- accessibility, figure resolution, color, fonts, and file-size requirements;
- bibliography format and supplement linkage.

For a doctoral dissertation, additionally audit:

- institution, program, degree type, dissertation form, and examination/deposit stage;
- required Chinese and English title, abstract, keywords, and semantic correspondence;
- cover, declarations, originality/authorization pages, contents and lists, chapter summaries, acknowledgments, publications, candidate contribution, and appendices as required;
- blind-review redaction versus final-deposit identity and metadata;
- included-publication version, attribution, co-author contribution, copyright, and permissions;
- repository submission format, accessibility, signatures, file naming, and archival checks.

If an official requirement cannot be verified, label it unresolved and do not claim compliance.
